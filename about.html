We're building a greener, cleaner, stronger valley.

Rootstock began with eleven acres of exhausted ley and a stubborn idea: that a farm could pay for the forest around it. Nine years later the farm feeds 900 households a week and the forest covers 1,840 acres.

Our story

Nine winters,
one idea.

We are not a charity that discovered farming, and not a farm that discovered marketing. We are a community interest company with an asset lock, a tractor, and 412,000 trees.

See what we planted

2016

Eleven acres and a borrowed spade

Two of us take on a failing dairy holding at Cwm Aeron. The first 3,000 trees go in that November, funded by a market stall.

2018

The nursery opens

We stop buying saplings and start growing them from seed collected within 40 miles. Cost per tree falls from £8.40 to £3.10.

2020

Boxes become forests

The veg box scheme reaches 500 households. Every subscription plants four trees a year — and always has.

2022

Neighbours join in

96 partner farms sign 100-year covenants, turning scattered corners into one connected corridor.

2026

Half a million in sight

412,000 trees planted, 94% surviving at year three, and a nursery capacity of 90,000 saplings a season.

How we work

Four practices, no shortcuts

Everything below is audited annually and published in our impact report. Where we fall short, we say so.

The carbon is in the roots

We stopped ploughing in 2017. Since then the top 30cm of our fields has gone from 3.1% to 5.4% organic matter — roughly 41 tonnes of extra carbon per hectare, held where it belongs.

  • Seven-year rotations with two years of herbal ley
  • No synthetic nitrogen since 2019
  • Compost made on-farm from green waste and woodchip
  • Sheep grazed on cover crops, never on young planting

Trees that already know this weather

Every seed is collected within 40 miles of where it will be planted, by hand, from trees we have mapped and monitored. Imported stock brings imported disease.

  • 27 native species, 90,000 saplings a year
  • Peat-free compost and reusable air-pruning trays
  • Two years in the nursery before planting out
  • Provenance recorded to a six-figure grid reference

Slow the water, hold the hill

Eleven miles of the Aeron now run through shade. Summer water temperature in the shaded reaches is 2.4°C lower, and the sediment load after heavy rain has halved.

  • Leaky dams and re-meandered ditches on 120 acres
  • Alder and willow carr on re-wetted peat
  • Fenced buffers of 12m either side of the main channel
  • Monthly invertebrate counts with the local rivers trust

Restoration is a job, not a gesture

Planting seasons employ 34 people from within 15 miles, on real contracts at the real living wage. Six apprentices a year train in nursery work, hedge-laying and coppice management.

  • Real Living Wage employer since 2019
  • Six paid apprenticeships each year
  • 1,900 volunteer days hosted last season
  • Free planting days for every local school
